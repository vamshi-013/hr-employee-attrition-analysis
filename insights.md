# HR Employee Attrition Analysis - Key Insights

## Overall Attrition

The dataset contains 1,470 employees.

Out of 1,470 employees, 237 employees left the company.

The overall employee attrition rate was 16.12%.

## Department Analysis

Sales had the highest attrition rate at 20.63%.

Human Resources had an attrition rate of 19.05%.

Research & Development had the lowest attrition rate at 13.84%.

This suggests that employee retention should receive more attention in the Sales department.

## Overtime Analysis

Employees working overtime had an attrition rate of 30.53%.

Employees not working overtime had an attrition rate of 10.44%.

This was one of the strongest patterns found in the analysis. Employees working overtime had almost three times the attrition rate compared to employees who did not work overtime.

## Job Role Analysis

Sales Representatives had the highest attrition rate at 39.76%.

Laboratory Technicians had an attrition rate of 23.94%.

Human Resources employees had an attrition rate of 23.08%.

Research Directors had the lowest attrition rate at 2.50%.

## Income Analysis

Employees who stayed had an average monthly income of 6,832.74.

Employees who left had an average monthly income of 4,787.09.

The results suggest that lower income may be associated with higher employee attrition.

## Job Satisfaction

Employees with Job Satisfaction level 1 had the highest attrition rate at 22.84%.

Employees with Job Satisfaction level 4 had the lowest attrition rate at 11.33%.

This suggests that employee satisfaction may have an impact on employee retention.

## Final Observation

The strongest factors observed in this analysis were overtime, job role, monthly income, and job satisfaction.

Sales Representatives and employees working overtime showed particularly high attrition rates. These areas could be investigated further to understand why employees are leaving.
